# Sanitized PRFAQ examples

These are fictional composites created to demonstrate the method. Product names, organizations, people, research, metrics, quotes, and timelines are synthetic. They are not claims about shipped work and should not be reused as evidence.

## Example 1: Community Circles

### Future press release excerpt

**Community Circles gives independent experts a private space to teach and connect with their most engaged followers**

**New membership tools combine focused discussion, structured learning, and direct expert access without forcing members into a separate experience.**

PORT CITY — A future launch date — Community Circles today introduced a membership experience for independent experts who want deeper, more durable relationships with their most engaged followers. Instead of scattering paid content, discussion, and audience management across unrelated services, experts can invite members into a focused space connected to the experience where those relationships already begin.

Independent experts often rely on public feeds that reward reach but make candid discussion, structured learning, and reliable access difficult. Followers who want more depth must navigate disconnected subscriptions, chats, and course tools. Community Circles lets an expert begin with one of three intentionally small models: a private discussion circle, a guided learning series, or scheduled group access. Members see the promise, price, participation expectations, and moderation rules before joining.

“The point is not to manufacture more content,” said the fictional product leader. “It is to give experts and their most committed followers a trustworthy place to create value together.”

### Internal FAQ excerpt

**Who is the initial customer?**  
Independent experts with an existing engaged audience and a repeated need for deeper interaction. The first user and seller are the same person; members are paying users and beneficiaries. The initial version excludes large organizations that require multi-admin workflows.

**What behavior are we trying to create?**  
Experts establish a clear recurring promise and return on a predictable cadence; members participate because the experience delivers access or learning they cannot receive from the public feed.

**What is the most contentious decision?**  
Whether to support three community models or begin with one. Breadth may improve creator fit, but it can obscure the product's core job and fragment learning. The pilot should use one shared infrastructure with constrained templates and compare repeat participation, moderation burden, and willingness to renew.

**What might disappoint the customer?**  
An empty space, unclear participation expectations, unsafe discussion, or a promise that depends on unsustainable creator effort. Activation should therefore measure member participation and creator follow-through, not merely community creation.

**Pilot gate:**  
Proceed only if the pilot demonstrates repeat participation, a manageable moderation load, and clear qualitative evidence that the private experience provides value distinct from public channels. Targets remain TBD until discovery establishes a credible baseline.

## Example 2: Operations Enrichment Assistant

### Future press release excerpt

**Operations Enrichment Assistant turns approved worklists into verified fulfillment-ready records**

**A bounded agent uses read-only data tools, deterministic reconciliation, and human review to replace repetitive lookup work without hiding missing or ambiguous records.**

LAKE CITY — A future launch date — Operations Enrichment Assistant today introduced a faster way for authorized operations teams to enrich approved worklists with information already available in governed company systems. A user supplies a spreadsheet, previews the requested fields and row population, and authorizes a read-only lookup. The assistant queries approved data through managed connectors, preserves every source row, flags missing or ambiguous matches, and creates a new reviewable output.

Teams frequently spend hours moving between spreadsheets and data tools to prepare routine handoffs. The work is repetitive, but mistakes can expose sensitive data or send an incomplete record downstream. The assistant automates the bounded lookup and reconciliation steps while keeping source population, permissions, exceptions, and final distribution visible to the user.

“Useful automation should reduce toil without creating invisible authority,” said the fictional operations leader. “The assistant can retrieve and reconcile; a person remains responsible for approving where the result goes.”

### Internal FAQ excerpt

**What is the agent's bounded job?**  
Read an explicitly supplied worklist, execute approved read-only queries, reconcile results to the source identifiers, and prepare a new output for review. It may not update source systems, infer missing sensitive values, share files, or send messages without separate authorization.

**What is authoritative state?**  
The source worklist defines the population. The governed query service defines available enrichment fields. The generated artifact is a review copy, not a new system of record.

**How will quality be evaluated?**  
Row preservation, identifier coverage, duplicate handling, query completeness, explicit missingness, provenance, and a read-back of the created artifact. A silent row drop or an unflagged ambiguous match fails the quality floor.

**What is the strongest counterargument?**  
A saved query and spreadsheet macro may solve the job with less complexity. The agent is justified only if source formats, approved queries, and exception handling vary enough that bounded reasoning materially reduces operator time without weakening controls.

**Pilot gate:**  
Continue only if the workflow reduces hands-on effort while meeting the quality floor, preserving human control over distribution, and operating within an agreed cost and latency budget. Baselines and thresholds must be measured during a shadow-mode pilot.
