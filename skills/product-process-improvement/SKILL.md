# Product Process Improvement

Improve the system of work without treating more ceremony as the default answer.

This is an original, public-safe operationalization inspired by licensed product-process materials from Ethan Tan / Exponentially.io and Pureplay Studios Ltd. It does not reproduce their process maps or worksheets.

## Define purpose and boundary

Name the user outcome the product system exists to create, the start and end of the process under review, the decision owner, and the period represented. Map a representative completed initiative or incident when possible rather than relying only on the documented process.

## Map the actual flow

Trace demand through discovery, decision, shaping, delivery, release, adoption, and verified outcome. Include only stages that actually occur. For each stage capture:

- input and exit evidence;
- owner and decision rights;
- active work time and wait time;
- queue, work in progress, and handoffs;
- information or artifact produced;
- rework, escalation, and failure demand; and
- feedback returning from users, operations, and product data.

Separate the official path, common shortcut, and exception path. Do not hide asynchronous work or stakeholder latency inside a generic “alignment” box.

## Diagnose the system

Look for:

- ambiguous entry or exit criteria;
- decisions without named owners or dates;
- large batches and long queues;
- repeated translation across teams or artifacts;
- discovery arriving after commitment;
- output completion without outcome verification;
- recurring incidents generated by earlier process choices;
- incentives that reward local throughput over system value; and
- missing, slow, or low-quality feedback loops.

Distinguish the constraint from its symptoms. Quantify elapsed time, active time, rework, or failure demand when sources support it; otherwise label the estimate.

## Design an improvement experiment

Generate remove, combine, clarify, automate, resequence, or add-feedback options. Select the smallest change likely to improve the system within one operating cycle while preserving necessary controls.

State the behavior change, beneficiary, new friction, baseline, primary process signal, user-outcome guardrail, owner, cost boundary, and stopping rule. Prefer a reversible trial with one team or work type before an organization-wide rollout.

## Required outputs

Deliver:

1. findings ordered by system impact;
2. a completed [Product Process Improvement Canvas](references/product-process-improvement-canvas.md); and
3. a 30-day or one-cycle experiment with a playback date.

## Quality gate

Confirm that the map shows actual rather than aspirational flow, includes wait and feedback, ties process performance to a user outcome, identifies the governing constraint, avoids ceremony without a mechanism, protects necessary risk controls, and defines read-back evidence for the experiment.

## Permission boundary

Do not schedule meetings, alter workflows, change issue states, or announce a process change without explicit authorization. Draft the experiment and stakeholder plan for review first unless the user requested execution.
