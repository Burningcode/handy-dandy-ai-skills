# Product Principles Decision Review

Turn product principles into decision instruments rather than slogans.

This is an original, public-safe operationalization inspired by licensed product-principles materials from Ethan Tan / Exponentially.io and Pureplay Studios Ltd. It does not reproduce their worksheets or examples.

## Choose the mode

- **Create:** infer candidate principles from strategy, repeated trade-offs, and consequential past decisions.
- **Critique:** test an existing principle set for distinctiveness, usefulness, and contradictions.
- **Apply:** use agreed principles to review a live product, portfolio, or operating decision.

## Build evidence before wording

Gather the strategy, target users, current constraints, examples of good and poor decisions, and tensions the team repeatedly faces. Label sources, inferences, and unknowns. Do not convert one leader's preference into an organizational principle without evidence of its intended scope and support.

## Define an operational principle

Each principle must include:

- the recurring decision tension it resolves;
- a directional choice that rules out a plausible alternative;
- where it applies and where it does not;
- observable behaviors or evidence of compliance;
- a counterexample or failure mode; and
- a review trigger when conditions change.

Use this form:

```text
When [recurring situation], prefer [direction] over [credible alternative]
because [user or strategic rationale], unless [boundary condition].
We will know we applied it when [observable decision or behavior].
```

A value such as “customer first,” a process rule such as “every ticket needs approval,” or an absolute with no exception is not yet a useful product principle.

## Resolve conflicts

Principles often collide. Name the precedence rule for the real context rather than pretending all principles can be maximized. If two principles always produce the same answer, combine them. If a principle never changes a decision, remove or rewrite it.

Keep the final active set small enough to remember and use. Maintain candidate or retired principles separately.

## Apply to a decision

For each viable option, record what each principle favors, the evidence, the tension introduced, and whether an exception is justified. Principles inform judgment; they do not replace user evidence, feasibility, viability, legal obligations, or executive accountability.

## Required outputs

Deliver:

1. findings first, including weak or conflicting principles;
2. an improved principle set or decision recommendation; and
3. a completed [Principle-to-Decision Canvas](references/principle-to-decision-canvas.md).

## Quality gate

Confirm that every retained principle:

- resolves a recurring, consequential trade-off;
- excludes a credible alternative;
- has a boundary and counterexample;
- can be observed in a decision or behavior;
- ties to user or strategic value; and
- can be revisited when evidence or context changes.

Do not invent consensus. Identify the owner who may adopt, amend, or retire the principle.
