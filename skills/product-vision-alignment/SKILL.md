# Product Vision Alignment

Create a credible shared picture of the future user transformation without smuggling in an untested strategy or feature list.

This is an original, public-safe operationalization inspired by licensed product-vision materials from Ethan Tan / Exponentially.io and Pureplay Studios Ltd. It does not reproduce their templates, boards, or examples.

## Distinguish the layers

- **Vision:** the durable future change for a defined user or ecosystem.
- **Strategy:** the choices and advantages used to move toward the vision.
- **Objective:** the nearer-term outcome and timeframe.
- **Roadmap or solution:** the sequence of bets and capabilities.

Push back when the proposed vision is a feature, revenue target, market category, or vague aspiration that could describe any company.

## Ground the future

Gather evidence about the target user, current journey, unmet need, alternatives, market or technology change, and organizational advantage. Separate facts, inferences, aspirations, and unresolved tensions. Include people who will build, operate, sell, support, or be affected by the future—not only the authoring leader.

## Choose the expression

Select the smallest artifact that can create the needed understanding:

- **Vision narrative:** for portable executive clarity.
- **Outcome canvas:** for comparing users, change, evidence, differentiation, and boundaries.
- **Future journey storyboard:** when context and sequence matter.
- **Experience prototype:** when words cannot reveal feasibility or emotional resonance.

Do not create every format by default. The required output is the narrative plus canvas; add a storyboard or prototype only when it materially reduces ambiguity.

## Write the vision

Make explicit:

- who experiences the change and who is outside the initial focus;
- the current struggle or constraint;
- the future behavior, capability, or condition;
- why that future is meaningfully better than alternatives;
- what remains true even if the first solution changes;
- the evidence supporting plausibility; and
- what the vision intentionally does not promise.

## Align through a playback

Present the user and current reality, walk through the future experience, then ask stakeholders to identify disagreement, missing evidence, unintended friction, and non-negotiable constraints. Record decisions and unresolved questions. Do not treat silence as agreement.

## Required outputs

Deliver:

1. a concise vision narrative;
2. a completed [Product Vision Alignment Canvas](references/product-vision-alignment-canvas.md); and
3. a playback plan with participants, decisions, and evidence gaps.

## Quality gate

Confirm that the vision is user-centered, durable across solution changes, differentiated, evidence-labeled, bounded, and understandable without internal jargon. Name the next strategic decision it enables and the evidence that could materially change it.
