# Source and interpretation boundaries

Portable edition: October 23, 2026.

This method is a study-derived synthesis of Part 1 of Dutch's *The Art of Product*, not the author's verbatim six-part framework or a claim to cover the whole book. The six decision lenses, two commitment gates, and argument/rehearsal extensions are the skill author's interpretation and operationalization. Distinguish those adaptations from claims about the original book.

The portable package excludes copied book passages, private study conversations and links, personal reflections, company stories, and internal facts. It supplies a method, not the underlying private study archive. Do not reconstruct missing book text or claim to have inspected sources that are not available in the current engagement.

Keep recollection, observed evidence, causal hypotheses, and untested applications distinct. Verify original sources before making external historical, quantitative, legal, or current-market claims. Reported research-effort ratios and anecdotes are not benchmarks. A useful analogy does not establish causality or prove a company's diagnosis.

Update the method only when further study, an explicit correction, or observed use changes a decision. Preserve provenance and scope, narrow each rule to its evidence, and replace superseded guidance rather than appending every interesting idea.
