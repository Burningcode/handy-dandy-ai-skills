# Art of Product Strategy

Improve the choice before polishing its defense. This is a reusable method, not a persona. It synthesizes a Part 1 study of Dutch's *The Art of Product*; the six lenses below are a study-derived synthesis, not the author's verbatim framework. Read [source-boundaries.md](references/source-boundaries.md) when discussing the book, provenance, or extending the method.

## Select the job

- **Decide:** clarify the actual choice, compare credible alternatives, recommend the next commitment.
- **Argue:** turn a supported choice into a clear executive narrative. Read [argument-and-casework.md](references/argument-and-casework.md).
- **Rehearse:** test the argument under realistic objections, then repair it. Read the same argument reference.

Infer the job from the request. Ask only for a missing fact that materially changes the work. For a quick exchange, use the consequential lens, not a six-section questionnaire. For a timed case, preserve its existing clock, collaboration gates, source restrictions, and required format; contribute the analytical lens without replacing that workflow.

## Decision discipline

Start with the decision owner, customer behavior to change, business consequence, constraints, and next commitment. Separate a commercial mandate, customer outcome, strategy, and implementation tactic. An outcome metric is not its own explanation.

For each decisive claim, trace: **observation → interpretation → credible alternative → distinguishing evidence → decision implication**. Keep reported experience, verified fact, inference, assumption, and unknown visibly distinct. A familiar story or convincing analogy cannot substitute for current evidence.

For a significant opportunity or investment, cover these two gates explicitly before recommending substantial commitment. Missing evidence can justify a bounded test, not manufactured certainty:

1. **Ground identification:** define the customer's job, current substitutes (including existing behavior), current position and direction of change, relevant organization-specific advantage, cost of winning and exiting. Include distribution, trust, capability, time, economics, and what customers must give up. An attractive market is not necessarily attractive ground for this organization. Explain the conditions in plain language; military categories are optional.
2. **Intelligence and contradictory evidence:** name the most consequential assumption, strongest contrary signal or missing disconfirmation, credible alternative explanation, representative sources, discriminating test, empowered decision owner, and what action the result would change. Absence of contradictory evidence is not confirmation. Use lawful, truthful, consent-respecting research.

Use the other lenses where they change the choice:

- **Fundamentals:** customer purpose and strategic belief, external timing, competitive position, leadership judgment, organizational ability to act. These correspond to Way, Heaven, Earth, Leader, and Law. Mark supported/challenged/unknown; avoid a decorative composite score.
- **Real problem and value:** separate customer decision, interface, and enabling capability. Identify buyer, user, beneficiary, and displaced value. Compare net customer benefit and business contribution, not activity alone. More data is useful only if it improves a consequential decision; accuracy itself can be the binding need.
- **Resource allocation and learning:** distinguish defending value, growth, differentiation, enabling capability, and learning options. Judge each against its intended job and a credible alternative use of the same resources. Define what stays unfunded. Fast shipping is not fast learning unless evidence changes decisions or allocation.
- **Adaptation and execution:** distinguish tactical adjustment, strategy change, and objective change. Identify authority, dependencies, legitimate safeguards, information routes, and escalation. Silence and delay are inquiry signals, not automatic diagnoses of culture.

## Make a choice

Compare the strongest feasible alternative, not a strawman. Include test, partner, wait, narrow, reposition, or decline when genuinely relevant; do not manufacture an exhaustive menu. Account for transition costs, incentive changes, second-order effects, and lost customer value. A valid need does not establish that a particular solution is winnable or economic.

Recommend one next commitment with confidence and conditions. For a material experiment, name the hypothesis, eligible population, primary metric and denominator/window, proposed or observed threshold, guardrails, owner, review point, and scale/revise/stop rule. With missing inputs, use explicit assumptions or symbolic economics rather than invented ROI. Explain what evidence would reverse the recommendation and who can act on it.

Default to a compact answer: choice and why; strongest evidence and uncertainty; real alternative; next test or commitment and decision request. Expand only to suit the artifact and stakes. Evidence-gathering, artifact drafting, and an approved strategy are not permission to execute, contact stakeholders, or publish.
